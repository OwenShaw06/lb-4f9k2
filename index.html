    Same pattern every week. Week 4 is a deload: one fewer set on every lift.

    Progression

    • Add one rep per set each week. When every set hits the top of the range, move to a harder version.
    • Push-ups: feet elevated, then archer.
    • Split squats and lunges: slower lowering, then a loaded backpack.
    • Pull-ups: work up to 4 × 7, then add a backpack or a 4-second lowering.
    • Skill holds (hollow body, L-sit, handstand): add a few seconds each week. Make the move harder only after you hit the top time on every set.
    • Stalled two weeks in a row? Check food and sleep before changing the program.
    • Week 8 retest: max pull-ups, push-ups, and body weight. Starting point: about 7 pull-ups, 20 push-ups, 7 dips, 150–160 lb.

    Gear to buy

    1. Doorway pull-up bar. Rated 300+ lb, on a solid door frame. Buy now.
    2. Parallettes, 8–12 inches tall. Dips, L-sits, deep push-ups.
    3. Loop resistance bands. Assisted pull-ups and warm-ups.
    4. Food scale and bathroom scale. They make the bulk measurable.
    5. Adjustable dumbbells. Later, only if legs stop progressing.

    Until the bar arrives, rows on a sturdy table cover pulling.

            Eat more

            Your gap is volume, not food quality. These make 2,800 calories easier.

            • Drink some of it. A glass of whole milk is about 150 kcal and 8 g protein, and it goes down faster than a meal. Two glasses a day add 300 kcal.
            • Add on top, do not swap. Peanut butter on toast (2 tbsp is 190 kcal), a tablespoon of olive oil on rice (120 kcal), cheese on anything (1 oz is 115 kcal and 7 g protein).
            • Blend a 700-calorie shake. 1 cup whole milk, 1 banana, 2 tbsp peanut butter, 1/2 cup dry oats, and 1 scoop protein powder. About 715 kcal and 45 g protein. Use it in place of the muffin. It is saved under Meals.
            • Fix lunch first. Snacks do not add up to a meal. 1.5 cups cooked rice, 4 oz chicken, 1 tbsp olive oil, and vegetables is about 665 kcal and 45 g protein. It is saved under Meals.
            • Protein at every meal. 30 to 40 g in each of four meals gets you to 140 g without one huge dinner.
            • Cook once for the week. Make rice and meat on Sunday and portion lunches into containers. Keep them refrigerated and eat them within 3 to 4 days.
            • Keep grab-and-go protein around. Greek yogurt, hard-boiled eggs (6 g and 70 kcal each), string cheese, canned tuna.
            • Short on calories late in the day? Peanut butter on 2 slices of bread plus a glass of milk is about 500 kcal and 23 g protein.

              About these numbers

              • Vitamin and mineral targets are US reference intakes for men 19–30. Fiber, sodium, and the sugar and saturated fat caps follow the same guidelines.
              • Calories start at about 2,800 for a lean bulk, with protein, carbs, and fat set around that. The Progress tab adjusts calories from your weekly weight.
              • US labels only list vitamin D, calcium, iron, and potassium besides the macros. For the rest, use the Foods that hit guide or look the food up in USDA FoodData Central.
              • Vitamin D is hard to reach from food alone in winter. Ask a doctor or pharmacist whether a supplement makes sense.
